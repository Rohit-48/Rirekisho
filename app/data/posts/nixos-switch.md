## The Problem With Every Other Distro

For years my setup was the same story on every machine: install the distro, spend a weekend installing packages, hand-edit a dozen config files, get everything just right, and then never touch it again out of fear. Reinstalling meant redoing all of it from memory, half-remembered `apt install` commands and dotfiles scattered across three different backup folders that never quite matched.

That's not a system. That's a house of cards you're afraid to breathe near.

## Just Living and Tinkering

**I am an atheist. I am also agnostic.**

That is not a contradiction. Atheism is the absence of belief in gods; agnosticism is the honest acknowledgment that we don't *know* for certain. Both can coexist, and for me, they do.

I don't follow the old scripts, not from society, not from religion, and not from any individual who demands my compliance. I am not my thoughts. I am not anyone's follower. I am one individual, and everything about my life will be decided by me, not by any person or external entity.

I live by the philosophy of **Absurdism**: life has no inherent meaning, the universe owes us no explanation, and the most honest response to that reality is to keep living anyway, fully, deliberately, and on your own terms. That's Camus' core idea, and it resonates with me.

---

I don't hate God. I simply don't have the energy to hate something.

What I take issue with is a particular kind of human mindset: the kind that stops questioning, follows blindly, and outsources its thinking to centuries-old belief systems without ever asking *why*. That mentality is the real issue, not faith itself, but the weaponization of it.

Here's the mechanism, and once you see it, you can't unsee it: **fear and desire**. That's the entire playbook.

Religious and social systems, especially the ones demanding unquestioned obedience, almost always operate by attaching their rules to something you're terrified of, or something you desperately want. Fear of punishment. Desire for reward. Hell or heaven. Shame or acceptance. They take ancient behavioral code and compile it directly into your mind before you're old enough to audit it.

Think of it like this: your mind is a system. Most people are running unreviewed packages from generations ago, beliefs installed without their consent and never garbage collected. It's like a NixOS config that's never had `nix-collect-garbage` run on it. Old derivations pile up, consuming space and shaping behavior.

You have to read your own config. Audit it. Keep what's genuinely yours. Remove what was simply handed down without reason.

---

What these systems often destroy, and this is the real cost, is **individuality**: the real you, the questioning you, the curious, conscious, prefrontal-cortex-using you.

The prefrontal cortex is the part of the brain responsible for critical thinking, decision-making, rational judgment, and long-term planning. It's what separates deliberate thought from instinctive reaction. Suppressing that through dogma, groupthink, and emotional manipulation effectively suppresses the most human part of being human.

History is full of individuals who thought differently and paid for it. I look at figures like **Buddha**, **Mahavira**, **Ashtavakra**, and **Swami Vivekananda** not as religious icons but as philosophers, thinkers who questioned their own time, often at great personal cost. Their societies weren't always kind to them either. That pattern repeats.

Society has always had a complicated relationship with the intelligent individual. It simultaneously celebrates thinkers in retrospect and resents them in the present.

*(And yes, I'm speaking specifically from the Indian context here. This is the container I live in: the pressures, the expectations, the particular flavor of social conformity. This is the water I swim in.)*

---

One thing I'll say about Indian millennials specifically: there's a strange confidence that emerged from the post-Independence generation. Parents raised during a time when the entire country was figuring itself out, economically, culturally, and politically, and somehow that uncertainty produced a certainty in their parenting. "In our time, we did this." But the context was completely different, and so was the country. Comparing those reference points to today is like comparing a monolith to a microservice. They're not the same architecture.

Millennials, in many ways, inherited that framing and passed it forward, slightly updated but fundamentally the same package. Gen Z is the first generation I've seen actually question the package itself. Yes, they're chaotic. Yes, some of it overshoots. But overcorrection after decades of suppression makes sense. That's not a bug; it's the system rebalancing.

---

The education system has one real flaw: it doesn't teach any of this. Critical thinking, epistemology, how to question, how to audit your own beliefs: none of it is covered. Everything else can be improved incrementally, but this omission is structural. You can patch a feature, but a missing foundation is a different kind of problem.

---

Here's the thing, though: you don't need the label "atheist." You don't need any label at all. Labels are just other people's categories, and the moment you adopt one, you're back in the same game of group identity.

You just need to live. Actually live.

Earn your money. Build something real. If you want a family, build it well. Raise children who can think. Respect the people around you. Respect the planet, nature, elements, and living systems. Be responsible for your choices, all of them, without exception.

You are already free. That's not a motivational quote; it's just true. The idea that freedom is something you earn by following the right path, reaching the right milestone, or getting permission from the right authority is the illusion. Society sells you a map to a destination you were already standing at.

Wake up, choom. Time to think.

---

Don't destroy your individuality. It's the most valuable thing you have, and it's the first thing every system, religious, social, or political, will try to take from you.

This is why I'm just living properly. Just living and tinkering.
